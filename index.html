Vietnam Workcamp 2026: Building skills, homes and friendships

Nairana and Mirrabooka Study Centres have a long tradition of encouraging university students to serve communities in less fortunate circumstances through practical workcamps. In 2026, the workcamp took place in Dong Kho, a village approximately three hours from Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Twenty volunteers participated in the project. The team included young men from Mirrabooka and Nairana Study Centres in Sydney, Glenrowan Study Centre in Auckland, and the United States. Before travelling, each volunteer was asked to raise $1,000 towards the project. Together, they raised $20,000 to fund the installation of a water-filtration unit for the village and the construction of a new home for a family living in difficult conditions.

Learning on the job

Working alongside local Vietnamese tradesmen, the volunteers dug foundations, poured concrete, laid bricks and rendered walls. Most had little or no previous construction experience, so the first few days involved a steep learning curve—and plenty of quality control from their more experienced Vietnamese colleagues.

The language barrier added another challenge. The local workers often attracted the volunteers’ attention by tapping their tools and then used gestures to demonstrate what needed to be done. Simple instructions could usually be understood, but more complicated tasks, such as moving scaffolding or learning how to lay bricks correctly, required patience and creativity on both sides.

At first, many of the volunteers struggled even to keep cement on a trowel. With time and repetition, however, they found their rhythm, improved their skills and became more adept at understanding their Vietnamese co-workers.

Connecting with the village

The construction site soon became a lively meeting place. Volunteers and local workers shared refreshments, laughed together and attempted—with varying degrees of success—to learn phrases in one another’s languages.

The presence of foreign volunteers also attracted the curiosity of the village children. During breaks, the volunteers spoke with them and played games of soccer. Some children later joined in by helping with small but meaningful jobs around the site. On one occasion, a group of boys aged about six to eight left their nearby fishing spot to help stack and move a pile of bricks.

Over the two weeks, these daily interactions enabled the volunteers to get to know the villagers and better understand their lives. They learned that a 14-year-old girl had stopped attending school, despite wanting to continue her education, because her family had no means of transport.

As funds remained from the original $20,000 raised, the volunteers were able to surprise her on their final day by taking her to a bicycle shop. She chose her own bicycle and can now look forward to returning to school.

A lasting contribution

The completed water-filtration system will improve access to clean water in a village that has relied on bottled water. The new brick house, twice the size of the family’s former tin home, will provide safer and more comfortable living conditions.

On their final morning in Dong Kho, the volunteers rose early and climbed the long flight of stairs to the Shrine of Our Lady of Ta Pao. There, together with local Catholics, they attended Mass and gave thanks for the experience. They then returned to Ho Chi Minh City for a few days of rest and sightseeing before travelling home.

The volunteers made a tangible and lasting contribution to Dong Kho, but the village also left a deep impression on them. They returned home tired, grateful and more aware of the difference that generosity, hard work and friendship can make.

Melbourne Cultural Trip: Friendship, formation and discovery

Nairana and Mirrabooka Study Centres helped a group of Year 9 and 10 students make good use of their winter holidays with a six-day cultural trip to Melbourne.

Twenty-four students travelled from Sydney, enjoying a road trip through picturesque parts of regional and rural Australia. For many of the participants, it was their first visit to Victoria’s capital.

One of the main purposes of the trip was to meet nine Year 9 and 10 students from Melbourne who are beginning to take part in the activities and formation offered by Drummond Study Centre. The gathering provided an excellent opportunity for the Sydney and Melbourne students to get to know one another and form new friendships.

During their stay, the group explored a variety of Melbourne landmarks and cultural attractions. These included Lygon Street and Our Lady Star of the Sea, a parish church entrusted to priests of Opus Dei. The program combined sightseeing and recreation with opportunities for personal development, shared prayer and Christian formation.

The students enjoyed spending time together in a positive and supportive environment. Existing friendships were strengthened, new ones were formed, and many participants joined in the norms of piety as a group—some for the first time.

After six full days of travel, cultural activities and companionship, the students returned home with many good memories and a stronger connection between the communities associated with Nairana, Mirrabooka and Drummond Study Centres.

Recollections

Recollections are a chance to step back from the whirlwind of daily tasks for around 2 hours of quiet prayer spent looking at God, the world, and ourselves. It includes a spiritual reading, a meditation given by a priest, examination of conscience, a talk, and benediction. The most important thing is to put yourself in front of our Lord, to look at Him and to let Him look at you.

The Education Development Association (EDA)

The Education Development Association (EDA) is a registered charitable public company, limited by guarantee, and was established on 4 November 1964, setting out to empower young people to achieve their full potential through the delivery of educational programmes focused on character building.

EDA owns and operates a number of Study Centres around Australia. The spiritual formation and faith-based activities at its Centres are entrusted to Opus Dei. EDA now has study centres in NSW, QLD, VIC, and co-ordinates activities nationwide.
